01 / Abstract

Low-bit quantization breaks the query–key interaction. CASK-Q calibrates for it directly.

Low-bit quantization perturbs the interaction between query and key projections, not just each projection separately. We introduce CASK-Q, a calibration objective for jointly optimizing scalar-quantized query/key weights using randomized sketches of row-centered causal attention-logit error. An exact prefix-sum identity evaluates the sketch in O(Ndr) arithmetic for sequence length N, head dimension d, and sketch width r, without constructing an N×N logit matrix. We characterize the estimator's mean and variance, prove a sharp global attention-divergence bound, and derive an exact O(Nd²) prefix-moment comparator. In a controlled 30-seed synthetic-attention experiment, CASK-Q reduces mean held-out attention-output normalized mean-squared error by 25.20% at nominal 2-bit and 28.13% at nominal 3-bit relative to independent query/key reconstruction, with identical serialized weight sizes and equal update counts.

02 / Results

Same bits. Same bytes. Less attention error.

Held-out attention-output NMSE over 30 synthetic teacher/data seeds (lower is better). CASK-Q matches the accuracy of dense joint calibration while never materializing the full attention-logit matrix.

03 / Method

How CASK-Q works.

01 / CENTER

Measure the error softmax can see.

The target is the causal attention-logit error with each row's mean removed — exactly the component softmax is invariant to is dropped.

02 / SKETCH

Probe it with shared random signs.

Teacher and student share fresh Rademacher probes. A prefix-sum identity computes the sketch in O(Ndr) without ever building an N×N matrix.

03 / CALIBRATE

Train Q and K together.

Gradients flow into both quantizers at once, so a perturbation in one projection can compensate for the other. Export stays plain scalar codes + FP16 scales.

The paper proves the estimator is unbiased with variance shrinking as 1/r, gives a sharp bound linking the centered error to attention KL divergence, and supplies an exact deterministic prefix-moment comparator. Twenty-six numerical tests check value and gradient agreement against the dense objective.

The implementation, experiment scripts, results and exported weights are open source: github.com/the-decentralized-compute-company/quantization.

04 / Scope

What the paper does — and doesn't — claim.

  • 01Experiments use controlled synthetic single-head attention teachers, not pretrained language models.
  • 02The baseline is independent Q/K reconstruction on the same quantizer — a controlled ablation, not GPTQ, GSQ or another published system.
  • 03Dense joint calibration reaches similar accuracy; CASK-Q's edge is avoiding the N×N matrix at long context.
  • 04Downstream perplexity, full-model compression and inference kernels are the next evaluation step.
