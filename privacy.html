Privacy

This website describes TDCC and runs the TDCC GPU marketplace. It collects only what the marketplace needs to work, and it has no analytics, advertising, or tracking cookies.

Marketplace accounts

When you create an account we store your name, your email address, and a salted hash of your password (never the password itself). A single session cookie keeps you signed in; it is HttpOnly, sent only over HTTPS, and expires after 30 days or when you sign out.

Listings and requests

Listings you publish (GPU model, memory, count, region, setting, price, description) and the first word of your name are public. Rental requests store what you enter: GPU count, hours, environment, access type, an optional SSH public key, and an optional message. Your email address and SSH key are shown to the other party only after a host accepts a request. If you link a TDCC node to a listing, the node reports its software version and the GPU names and memory it detects about once a minute; this is shown on the listing. If the host turns on tdcc API rentals, the node also sends its mesh invite token (its network address); it is shown only to renters whose request was accepted, while their rental lasts. A renter who chooses tdcc API access gives their tdcc owner ID, a public-key fingerprint that the host's machine uses to admit them for the rented hours.

Emails we send

We email you only about your account: a link to confirm your address, a password reset link when you ask for one, and notices when a rental request is sent, accepted, declined, or cancelled. Links in these emails are single-use and expire (48 hours for confirmation, one hour for password reset). Emails are delivered through Resend, which processes your address and the message for that purpose only. If you report a listing, the report and your name and email are visible to our moderators.

Retention and deletion

You can delete your account at any time from your account page. That removes your profile, listings, and request history. Short-lived rate-limit counters keyed by IP address are kept for at most a day to stop abuse.

Server logs

The hosting provider may process standard technical logs such as IP address, browser type, timestamps, and requested pages for security and reliability.
