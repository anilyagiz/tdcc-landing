The verified execution layer for open AI.

TDCC connects AI companies, data centers, and independent GPU operators so people can train, fine-tune, and run inference on capable infrastructure through one dependable interface.

01 / Thesis

AI demand is growing faster than dedicated capacity.

Modern AI teams need flexible access to inference, evaluation, synthetic data, and fine-tuning capacity. Yet infrastructure remains fragmented across hyperscalers, specialist data centers, research labs, and underutilized GPUs.

TDCC coordinates that capacity into a verified execution network. The goal is not to expose a raw marketplace. The goal is to make distributed infrastructure feel like a reliable product: submit work, receive an accepted result, and pay for useful compute.

02 / Mission

One network for the complete model lifecycle.

For AI companies

Access flexible capacity for model training, fine-tuning, batch inference, evaluation, and production workloads without managing every machine directly.

For data centers

Expose suitable GPU capacity to qualified demand with workload-aware scheduling, utilization controls, and a path to recurring compute revenue.

For GPU operators

Contribute compatible hardware and earn from accepted jobs while reputation and reliability history improve placement over time.

For developers

Use a conventional API and execution primitives instead of building bespoke orchestration for heterogeneous infrastructure.

03 / Network design

Distributed capacity, coordinated by verification.

01

Declare capacity

Hosts register hardware, location, availability, runtime support, pricing, and reliability constraints.

02

Submit a workload

An AI company or developer submits a training, fine-tuning, inference, or evaluation job through the TDCC interface.

03

Match and execute

Model compatibility, VRAM, performance, latency, price, and reputation inform placement on a host or certified cluster.

04

Verify and settle

Workload-specific checks validate the result. Accepted work is recorded, reputation is updated, and settlement can be initiated.

Design principle: TDCC is output-first. Customers pay for accepted AI work, not for an unmanaged allocation of machine time.

04 / Product scope

From training experiments to production inference.

The initial product direction prioritizes workloads that can be scheduled and verified across heterogeneous infrastructure.

Training and fine-tuning

Support for distributed training where appropriate, plus LoRA and QLoRA adapter workflows for efficient specialization of open models.

Inference and serving

Batch and online inference, adapter serving, image generation, embeddings, and open-model hosting on compatible nodes.

Evaluation and data

Model evaluations, synthetic data generation, audio transcription, and reproducible benchmark workloads with result verification.

Agent workloads

Composable execution for AI agents that need dependable access to models and tools without owning the underlying hardware.

05 / Roadmap

A practical path from coordination to network scale.

Phase I · FoundationDefine workload schemas, host requirements, secure runtimes, scheduling primitives, and verification policies.
Phase II · Pilot networkConnect selected AI companies and data centers. Validate training, fine-tuning, inference, and evaluation workflows with measurable service levels.
Phase III · Capacity expansionOnboard additional data centers and qualified GPU operators, improve routing, introduce reputation signals, and expand geographic resilience.
Phase IV · Production platformOffer a stable developer API, observability, billing, automated settlement, and enterprise-grade controls for sustained AI workloads.

07 / Risks and principles

Trust is earned through measurable execution.

The network must address unreliable hosts, malicious behavior, model and runtime incompatibility, privacy requirements, energy economics, latency, and disputes over completed work. TDCC will prioritize workload isolation, clear acceptance criteria, auditability, reputation, failover, and transparent limits over unsupported promises.
